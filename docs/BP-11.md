# BP-11 - tactical characters and animation

## Migration and model

The previous player was a single instanced pool of rectangular body parts,
with head pitch and simple shoulder/hip swings. First-person hands were four
static colored boxes embedded in the weapon meshes. There was no Three.js
skeleton or articulated elbow/knee chain.

The migration keeps the movement capsule, prediction, weapon controller,
camera, weapon models, sight transforms, ammunition and damage formula. A shared
procedural character definition now supplies the remote model and authoritative
animated hitboxes. The existing weapon root receives a separate articulated arm
mesh. HOLO-01 retains its established stationary calibration geometry and damage
sensors; the tactical soldier is the multiplayer player model. The new arms
appear in both training and multiplayer.

The soldier has a proportionate head and neck, a broad chest/waist, sturdy jointed
limbs and shaped feet. Its uniform includes a plate carrier, three magazine pouches,
gloves, knee/shoulder protection, combat boots, helmet with rails, goggles,
face wrap, belt, backpack, radio and unit patch. Desaturated olive, grey and teal
colors preserve team readability and the existing retro palette. The attached
weapon has receiver, barrel, stock and magazine geometry.

All 39 pieces reuse one convex, eight-sided tapered primitive with outward flat
normals and 48 triangles. This gives rounded silhouettes without smoothing the
pixel presentation or adding subdivisions. One soldier is 1,872 triangles;
eight soldiers fit in the existing single instanced draw. Material, geometry
and color buffers are reused; colors only upload when appearance changes.
Characters add no lights or shadow maps.

## Skeleton and animation

`characterRig.ts` defines 22 parented joints: pelvis, spine, chest, neck, head,
both shoulders, upper arms, forearms and wrists, both hips, thighs, shins and
ankles, and a chest-space weapon target followed by both hands. The client constructs
real `THREE.Bone` hierarchies and `THREE.Skeleton` instances. Rigid skinning binds
each faceted segment to its joint and writes the resulting matrices into the
shared instance pool. Eight skeletons are pooled and reused.

The server evaluates the same hierarchy with small quaternion/translation
helpers, without importing Three.js. Tests compare every resulting joint to the
Three.js world matrix, including combined aiming, reload, slide and hit poses.
Limb dimensions remain constant when posture changes; lowered states bend joints
and reposition the pelvis instead of vertically squashing the body.

| State               | Character motion                                                                           |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Idle                | Breathing through pelvis/chest/shoulders and a relaxed two-handed grip.                    |
| Walking             | Distance-driven alternating hips/knees, ankles, pelvis and torso counter-motion.           |
| Running             | Stride amplitude and cadence rise continuously with actual travel speed.                   |
| Sprinting           | Stronger stride, forward torso lean, arm carry and attached weapon movement.               |
| Jumping             | Blended airborne posture with asymmetric hip/knee tuck.                                    |
| Landing             | Brief additive pelvis/knee compression with exponential recovery.                          |
| Sliding/backsliding | Low seated hips, one extended leading leg, one deeply folded trailing leg, and level head. |
| Diving/recovery     | Forward prone lean with continuous transition back to standing.                            |
| Reloading           | Support arm reaches across the magazine while the firing hand retains the weapon.          |
| Damage              | Brief direction-dependent torso/head reaction and a short readable color flash.            |

The game retains its walking/sprinting movement states. Running is the faster
part of the continuous gait blend, rather than a new movement mode. Gait advances
from actual capsule displacement during simulated ticks, so a blocked player
stops cycling their legs. Animation weights use exponential damping; slide-jump,
landing, reload cancellation and hit recovery blend independently. Interrupted
reloads retain their last phase while the reload weight fades. ADS suppresses
sprint arm carry.

`CharacterAnimation` owns fifteen presentation values, including the two
previous gait values. The server publishes phase/time, stance weights, ADS,
reload phase/weight and directional hit reaction in Colyseus player state.
Remote interpolation blends them with the existing transform history and
handles wrapped angles. Animation resets with a new life. Clients cannot provide
these values through the input protocol.

Damage reactions are queued for the next simulation tick. All shots in the
current tick therefore validate against one consistent pose, preserving trades
and preventing an earlier shot from moving the target's hitboxes mid-tick.
These poses never modify camera input, movement impulses or weapon cooldowns.

## First-person arms and weapon alignment

`FirstPersonArms` provides six bones and two-bone inverse kinematics. Sleeve,
forearm, cuff, palm, thumb and finger shapes use sixteen pooled instances in one
additional draw. Both hands reach the existing weapon-local grip points. The
left hand follows the existing damped reload pose toward the magazine; the
right hand remains on the firing grip.

The arm mesh inherits the original weapon root. Existing ADS offsets, rifle
red-dot collimation, SMG notch, shotgun bead, sniper raise/eye position, recoil,
equip lowering, sprint carry and scope styles retain their transforms. Classic
scope visibility also hides the arms with that root. Offline pause freezes arm
updates. The arms reuse the current weapon material and nearest-filtered pixel
texture, with UVs on the new geometry. No new camera shake or input smoothing
was added.

## Hit validation and compatibility

The new rig has 22 oriented collision hulls representing the same eleven BP-10
damage regions. Upper arm/forearm share the corresponding arm region; thigh/calf
share the corresponding leg region. Helmet, goggles and face wrap count as head;
the carrier counts as upper torso. Backpack, pouches, radio and gun are cosmetic.
Each collision hull follows its joint's complete translation and quaternion.
The closest valid hit still resolves behind world cover and through existing
server ownership/fire-rate checks. Body multipliers, range falloff, pellet
aggregation, friendly blocking, protection and kill rules are unchanged.

Compatibility checks cover the four weapons, sniper balance, both scope styles,
camera/firing sensitivity, automatic/manual/interrupted reloads, sprinting,
sliding, slide jumps, diving, multiplayer respawn and private room loadouts/caps.
The existing stationary hologram tests remain intact, including scoped arm/leg
damage checks.

## Asset sources

The character, rig, animation poses, gear and first-person arm geometry are
authored procedurally in this repository for BP-11. They use the existing Three.js
dependency, readable material and generated pixel texture. There are no imported
character assets, motion-capture clips, external downloads, new dependencies or
third-party asset licenses to track.

## Slide pose refinement

The slide now settles into a feet-first seated skid: the hips sit about 24 cm
above the capsule foot anchor, the leading leg extends forward, the trailing
knee folds beneath the body, and the ankles counter-rotate to level the boots.
The torso reclines about 32 degrees while the neck/head counter-rotate to keep
the gaze forward. Pelvis lowering follows leg clearance, so entry and recovery
do not drag the soles through the ground. The server evaluates these same poses
for hit validation.

In first person, the weapon settles lower and slightly closer into a brace with
lowered elbows. Slide roll is reduced from about 10 to 3 degrees. The extra brace
fades completely in ADS, preserving sight and scope alignment, and IK maintains
both grip points. Slide speed, friction, momentum, duration, capsule behavior and
slide-jump rules are unchanged. No geometry, draw calls or animation fields were
added. A close-view capture is `test-results/slide-enhanced.png`.

Follow-up verification: all 406 tests across 49 files and 18 Chrome scenarios
pass, including the close slide view, networked poses, seven movement scenarios,
both sniper scope styles, limb hits and sprint carry. New checks cover sole
clearance across slide blend weights, feet-first geometry, level gaze, both hand
grips during the brace, and unchanged full ADS alignment for all four weapons.

## Third-person weapon handling

Other players now see a two-handed ready pose, a muzzle-up sprint carry,
shoulder-height ADS, weapon pitch following the player's look direction,
confirmed-shot recoil and a support-hand reload reach. These poses blend with
walking, jumping, sliding, diving and recovery. The sprint pose uses the same
0.9/0.22/-0.25 radian pitch/yaw/roll targets as the first-person carry. Aiming,
firing, recent shots, equipping and reloading suppress that carry so the remote
weapon returns to a firing position during those actions.

The weapon target is attached to the chest. Shared analytic two-bone IK places
both palms at weapon-local grips, with outward/downward elbow poles and fixed
28/26 cm arm segments. The firing hand stays attached throughout the reload;
the support hand leaves and returns as the existing reload phase blends. The
same solved joints drive Three.js rendering and authoritative arm/hand hitboxes.
The weapon remains cosmetic for hit validation. Character part counts, geometry,
draw calls, movement and first-person handling are unchanged.

Two additional server-owned floats, `carryBlend` and `fireBlend`, pass through
the Colyseus schema, snapshot copy and interpolation. Recoil is triggered only
by accepted server shots, and decays exponentially. Animation reset clears both
values on respawn. Restart the room server and refresh all browsers together
after updating the schema.

## One-handed sprint refinement

Sprint carry now keeps the right hand on the weapon and releases the left arm
into an alternating forward/back swing. Both views use the same swing curve,
distance cadence (pi/1.8 radians per meter), and 12/18-per-second entry/exit
damping. The server carry now targets the actual sprint condition directly,
removing the extra delay from damping an already-damped sprint weight. Walking
also advances the shared cadence so the transition into sprint stays continuous.

In first person the free arm moves in camera space, independently of the raised
weapon. The remote arm uses the same upper-arm/elbow/wrist angles in its body
hierarchy. Quaternion blends release and recover the support hand while keeping
segment lengths constant. Aim, fire/recent shots, reload, equip, traversal and
stopping restore the established handling. Reduced motion scales first-person
swing amplitude. Remote movement remains interpolated with the existing network
delay; matching the action and cadence does not remove network latency.

The existing `carryBlend` and `gaitPhase` synchronize the remote presentation;
no new schema fields, geometry, draw calls or dependencies are needed. Shared
evaluation keeps the swinging arm's hitboxes aligned with its rendered pose.
Restart the room server and refresh connected browsers to load both updated views.

The changes are in shared `weaponHandling.ts`, `characterRig.ts` and
`characterAnimation.ts`, plus client `FirstPersonArms.ts` and `WeaponViewmodel.ts`.
New/updated tests cover one-handed holds for all four weapons, full look range,
free-arm motion and cycle continuity, unchanged firing grip, fixed arm lengths,
