# BP-12 - barrel-origin trails and ADS transparency

Implemented one pooled tracer renderer for training and multiplayer. Each shot
starts at its weapon's animated barrel attachment. ADS smoothly lowers opacity,
width and length; sniper aiming uses a still subtler profile. All four weapon
types, both sniper scope styles and the existing low-resolution renderer are
supported.

## Muzzle attachments

Coordinates below are model-local meters, `(x, y, z)`. Forward is negative Z.

| Weapon  | First-person muzzle | Remote weapon muzzle |
| ------- | ------------------- | -------------------- |
| Rifle   | `(0, 0.025, -0.53)` | `(0, 0.055, -0.65)`  |
| SMG     | `(0, 0.025, -0.38)` | `(0, 0.055, -0.47)`  |
| Shotgun | `(0, 0.025, -0.70)` | `(0, 0.055, -0.79)`  |
| Sniper  | `(0, 0.025, -1.06)` | `(0, 0.055, -1.10)`  |

`WeaponMuzzles.ts` defines the individual barrel lengths and exits. First-person
attachments are named `muzzle-rifle`, `muzzle-smg`, `muzzle-shotgun` and
`muzzle-sniper`, parented to their respective weapon meshes. The sniper exit
includes its muzzle brake. Muzzle flashes now use these same exits.

World positions are resolved after camera, weapon and remote skeleton updates.
The first-person calculation accounts for the separate 60-degree viewmodel
camera: its projected barrel position is mapped into the world/peripheral
camera's FOV, then rotated and translated into world space. This preserves the
visible barrel/trail join through ADS, recoil, reload, sprint, slide and dive.
Remote barrels use their actual animated weapon-bone matrices; their lengths
now vary with the equipped weapon without adding geometry or draw calls.

## Appearance and sight protection

All tuning is in `apps/client/src/game/effects/tracerConfig.ts`.

| Profile       | Base opacity | Thickness | Maximum visual length | Protected center radius |
| ------------- | -----------: | --------: | --------------------: | ----------------------: |
| Hip fire      |          52% |   0.012 m |                 5.0 m |                    none |
| ADS           |          14% |   0.005 m |                 2.5 m |      0.035 vertical NDC |
| Scoped sniper |           8% |   0.003 m |                 1.6 m |      0.060 vertical NDC |

The existing continuous ADS blend drives a smoothstep interpolation between
profiles, including effects already alive when ADS changes. Lifetime is 75 ms,
with fading and a subdued warm color. These are base opacities; lifetime,
longitudinal taper and center protection reduce individual fragment alpha.

The shader fades incoming and outgoing trails around the reticle center while
retaining visible segments outside it. Aspect correction is updated for each
world/optic render camera. Trails are depth-tested, do not write depth, add no
glow/bloom, and render before the weapon/HUD. Pixel textures, nearest filtering,
native HUD rendering, sights, reticles and scope composition keep their behavior.

## Trajectory and multiplayer

The existing hitscan resolver remains the source of truth. A short cosmetic
bridge joins the barrel to the validated eye-origin ray over up to 0.65 m of
forward ray distance; the rest of the visible segment lies on that original
ray. This reconciles the model's offset with eye-origin aiming without redirecting
the bullet or inventing a different hit. The visual ends at or before the real
hit/range endpoint. Hits closer than the barrel do not draw backward through
the surface. An emitted trail stays fixed in world space rather than following
the gun afterward.

Training supplies its existing hit/miss endpoints through an optional visual
callback. Multiplayer queues only server-confirmed traces; predicted weapon
animation does not create trails. Bounded server-shot ID tracking rejects
duplicate notifications before impacts/trails/audio are repeated. Remote effects
wait the existing 100 ms interpolation delay so their origins match displayed
characters. A missing muzzle or a late confirmation for a stowed weapon is skipped
rather than emerging from the eye or the wrong weapon.

`Game` owns the single shared trail pool. It is cleared on timing/session resets
and disposed with the game. The pool holds 128 rays, at most two segments per
ray, one reusable box geometry, material and instanced draw. Expiry returns slots
to the pool; no geometry/material allocation or particle system runs per frame.
Diagnostics expose active/emitted counts and current visual profile as data
attributes without adding a new in-game settings flow.

## Verification

- **435 automated tests across 52 files pass.** Muzzle coordinates touch actual
  geometry faces; projected barrel positions agree across separate cameras;
  animation transforms update exits; remote exits match rendered barrel faces;
  rays/endpoints remain unchanged; pooling, expiry, duplicate suppression and
  continuous nonzero ADS profiles are covered.
- **34 Chrome scenarios pass.** These include all four tracers at 640x360 and
  1920x1080, continuous ADS firing, both sniper scope styles, sights, reloading,
  sensitivity, seven movement scenarios, character animation, network damage,
  enemy occlusion and two eight-player performance runs.
- **One additional GPU pixel check passes.** For a trail crossing camera center,
  the central 3x3 pixels contain zero tracer energy in ADS and scope modes while
  224/111 pixels remain lit elsewhere (hip: 544 lit pixels). A foreground wall
  produces zero pixel difference with/without the trail, and expiry leaves zero
  tracer energy. This checks actual shader output and depth behavior.
- Build/typecheck, lint and formatting pass. No server hit-validation, damage,
  weapon timing, movement or sensitivity implementation was modified.

Native GPU: Chrome 157.0.8103.79, Intel UHD Graphics 620 / D3D11, Core i7-8650U,
1280x720 viewport. One viewer plus seven moving/aiming/firing/auto-reloading SDK
actors remained connected. Each run sampled 15 seconds after a three-second
warmup; AA and adaptive resolution were off.
